Agricultural output and productivity growth have slowed across many of the world’s major growing regions as rising temperatures, 
changing rainfall patterns, and more frequent extremes constrain production systems. 

While substantial research documents climate impacts on yields and productivity, less is known about the capacity of agricultural systems 
to adjust their underlying production structures to adjust to climate variability. 

This study examines system‑level adjustment to climate variability over 1961-2021 in four major non‑irrigated agricultural regions: 
North America, Central Europe, Sub‑Saharan Africa, and Australia. 

Using cointegration analysis and vector error‑correction models, we distinguish short‑run climate‑driven deviations from long‑run 
equilibrium relationships linking temperature anomalies and water‑availability conditions to agricultural productivity and key production components. 

We find pronounced asymmetries in adjustment capacity. 

Total factor productivity exhibits equilibrium‑correcting behaviour in most regions, indicating partial recovery following climate related shocks. 

In contrast, adjustment in aggregate agricultural production is substantially more constrained. Structural components, particularly livestock production 
and capital‑intensive inputs, adjust slowly and, in some regions, display persistent declines following adverse climate conditions. By comparison, land‑use 
composition and variable input use remain relatively flexible margins of adjustment.

These results suggest that productivity adjustments increasingly stabilise agricultural systems rather than sustain long‑run production 
growth under climate stress.
